Living New Deal

This repository contains data for the Living New Deal website. Our website can be found at https://livingnewdeal.org. Our data consists of information about physically located sites that were created under the aegis of the New Deal. We endeavor to collect information for each site regarding the New Deal Agency it was funded by, years the project began and ended, the site's location, its present use if it still exists, as well as other metadata. The sources used in verifying this information vary, but often the National Archive, city and county records, and contemporary newspaper articles are cited. Each verified New Deal site appears on our interactive national map at https://livingnewdeal.org/map. As of June 25, 2026, there are 19,501 New Deal sites on that map, they are shown in this .csv export of our database.

The Living New Deal’s mission is threefold: to document the extraordinary material contributions of the New Deal, 1933-1942, to every city, state and territory of the United States; to educate the American public about the New Deal and its long-term impacts; and to keep alive the New Deal vision of large-scale government programs to meet the nation’s challenges and serve the common people.

Our first goal is to build the only comprehensive database of New Deal public works and artworks, a unique digital humanities resource. Site data are assembled through a nationwide crowdsourcing network, vetted by experienced staff and presented on an interactive online map of the country. We hold a huge repository of photographic, geographic, economic, demographic, and biographical information. All our resources are freely available to scholars, librarians, officials and the general public.

Our second goal is to educate the young and old about the New Deal’s achievements (as well as its shortcomings). We are showing Americans that their lives are still shaped by the New Deal: from sweeping policies like social security, farm supports, and labor rights to local schools, bridges, parks and waterworks still in everyday use. Our comprehensive website, virtual programs and in-person events connect experts and enthusiasts to a broad range of students, teachers, scholars, journalists, and officials for a critical appreciation and discussion of this vital epoch in American history.

Our third goal is to demonstrate that the New Deal’s programs, long-term investments, aid to working people and sense of social responsibility make it a vital model for public policy today. In a time of division, we are illustrating how the New Deal rebuilt the country in the face of economic calamity, helped millions of desperate working families, and restored faith in democracy. Government today cries out for the spirit of the New Deal: investing in the public good, engaging communities large and small, and building a broad popular coalition for change.

Yes, the American past is flawed and the New Deal with it, so our mission is to present this epoch’s history honestly and to correct mischaracterizations that blind Americans to its inspiring values and political leaders to its practical lessons for today. In short, we believe America needs a new New Deal crafted for the twenty-first century.
